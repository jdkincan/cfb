Read this the right way

This is a construction, not a prediction. The most common exact seeded bracket across 20,000 simulations turned up 0.005% of the time — once. Even ignoring seeding entirely, the most common set of twelve appeared in 0.01% of seasons. There are more plausible brackets than there are simulations to hold them.

So the bracket above is not the future. It is the centre of a very wide distribution, and its value is in the shape: who is on the bye line, who is fighting for the last spots, and which quarterfinal is a coin flip.

Two things the model is probably wrong about. It has Texas Tech at 11 despite a 9.5-win Big 12 title, because the committee model docks Big 12 schedule strength hard — the real committee had Texas Tech 4th in 2025 while this model had them 7th. And it will understate blue bloods on the bubble generally: it counts losses, while the committee also weighs who you are.

The genuinely informative bits: Notre Dame projects the most wins in the country (10.4) on a soft schedule but has no conference, so it can only ever be an at-large. Oregon versus Texas in the quarterfinal is a literal coin flip at 50%. And the 8–9 and 7–10 games are close enough that home field is doing most of the work.
